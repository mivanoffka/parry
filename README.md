# Parry — 2.5D movement and melee prototype

Open `Assets/Scenes/SampleScene.unity` in Unity 6000.6.3f1 and press Play.
Scroll the mouse wheel up to zoom in or down to zoom out. Zoom eases smoothly toward the selected scale and stops at minimum/maximum limits. This also works in the AI duel, where manual zoom adjusts the automatic framing.
For an AI-versus-AI spectator level, open `Assets/Scenes/AIDuel.unity` and press Play. The crimson and azure enemies fight each other using the same combat AI, automatically respawn after defeat, and share a camera that keeps both in view. Their positions, stats and opponent references are saved in the scene and editable in the Inspector. This scene is also included in Build Settings.
The `Parry Gameplay Demo` scene object contains the saved arena, player, five training dummies, active melee enemy, fill lights and feedback volume. They are visible and editable before Play. Generated character/shadow sprites, materials and the feedback profile are saved in `Assets/Art/Generated`. Play initializes combat, weapons, indicators and spark emitters without recreating the arena.

Edit positions, obstacle sizes, lighting and fighter settings directly in the scene Inspector and save the scene. `Parry > Bake Arena Into Scene` is a one-time migration for an empty arena root; on an already populated scene it validates the saved content without rebuilding it. The editor utility also supports batch validation via `Parry.Editor.ArenaSceneBuilder.Run` and a Play Mode initialization check via `Parry.Editor.ArenaSceneBuilder.VerifyRuntime` (run without `-quit`).

The migrated scene passes Unity batch checks for saved asset references, one player, one enemy, five dummies, seven solid arena objects and Play Mode initialization without duplicate arena/feedback objects. This was checked in a temporary project copy because the main project was already open. ## Sword styles and powerful attacks

Press **1** for the normal sword mode (slash and block/parry; no strong attack). With at least one combat warmup charge, press **2** to enter the first preparation phase for one powerful lunge: the sword points forward, and this mode has **no normal attack**. Preparation immediately adds a faint white weapon gleam, drains **5 concentration points per second** while waiting (including while moving), and automatically returns to normal after **5 seconds** or when the drain is unaffordable. Repeatedly pressing 2 while prepared does not restart the timer. Waiting prevents passive concentration recovery; its cost is separate from the 390-point attack cost and stops when the attack starts. Tune `preparationPressurePerSecond` in the Inspector. Pressing 2 immediately begins the 0.9-second windup, then holds the drawn-back pose. Once this windup is complete, running speed increases by **50%** while the lunge remains prepared (12 units/second at the default speed of 8). The bonus ends on attack, cancellation, timeout or interruption. Tune `preparedRunSpeedMultiplier` in the Inspector. Click LMB / press J / gamepad X once to release the strike without a second windup; no hold is required, and releasing does not trigger another attack. An early click queues the strike until the current windup finishes, locking its aim at the click. After the lunge finishes (including recovery), is interrupted, or the player raises guard, the player automatically returns to mode 1. Raising guard also cancels a committed powerful lunge. Preparation is cleared by a roll, Escape, taking damage, concentration disruption, focus loss, disable or respawn. Style selection is accepted while idle; another powerful lunge requires pressing 2 again. Aim locks when attack is pressed. Ordinary slashes retain their short recovery input buffer; powerful lunges cannot queue another attack while executing.

A powerful lunge costs **390 concentration points**, paid once when the prepared strike is released, even if it later misses or is interrupted. If 390 or fewer points remain, the strike still executes and consumes the remaining reserve. Exhaustion applies the usual 1.25-second balance break immediately after the damaging thrust ends, or when the committed strike is interrupted. It does not cancel the strike before damage. Affordable strikes do not trigger this penalty; an existing stagger still prevents attacking. Cancelled preparation does not pay the strike cost. Tune `PlayerMotor.strongAttackPressure` in the Inspector.

The powerful lunge has a 0.9-second preparation windup started by key 2, a 0.12-second thrust with 1.4 units of collision-limited forward travel, and 0.32 seconds of recovery. The powerful windup starts with one subdued white flash: it rises in roughly 0.06 seconds and fades by 0.36 seconds at the default windup duration. The held pose keeps only a tiny dim glint, without a blade halo or nearby light. All charge glow disappears when the strike begins. Cancelling the charge immediately clears the gleam. The white gleam warns that a powerful lunge is being charged. The normal idle stance holds the sword raised for slashes; the thrusting stance points it forward along the aim direction. It can be dodged, blocked or parried. Blocking or parrying a lunge pushes both fighters apart: the defender along the incoming strike and the attacker backward, at an initial speed of 12. Both defenses stop the attacker’s forward thrust with recoil; movement still respects walls. A block protects health and retains its usual 28-point defender cost, but always breaks the defender’s balance for 1.25 seconds regardless of remaining concentration. A successful parry costs the defender **35% of maximum endurance, with a minimum of 80 points** (without a forced balance break), applies the same two-sided push, and adds the usual attacker recoil and 45-point pressure penalty. The powerful lunge uses a narrow directional lane even with circular attacks enabled and advances the player. Walls obstruct movement and hits. Each target can be hit once per attack. Rolls have no automatic invulnerability. During a roll, both fighters use a separate damage capsule with **half the standing radius and 60% of the standing height**, keeping its bottom at the same level. Melee checks intersect this capsule against the weapon’s actual reach and sector/lunge lane. A strike that intersects the smaller shape still deals damage and interrupts the roll; a missed reduced hitbox does not consume the swing. Movement collision against walls stays full-size. The damage capsule restores when the roll ends or is interrupted. `WeaponStyleOptions` declares the optional normal attack, strong attack and guard action for each sword style.

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard / mouse | Gamepad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move | WASD / arrow keys | Left stick |
| Aim | Mouse on the ground plane | Right stick |
| Relaxation stance | Alt / Option | — |
| Sword preparation | 1: normal / 2: prepare powerful lunge | — |
| Attack | Left mouse button / J | West button (X / Square) |
| Block / timed parry | Hold / tap RMB or K | Hold / tap left shoulder (LB / L1) |
| Roll | Space | South button (A / Cross) |
| Revive / heal and return to center | R | — |

Player movement speed is 8 units/second (previously 5.5), acceleration is 48 and roll speed is 15. Enemy movement speed is 4.2 (previously 3.1).

Movement uses a 3D CharacterController on XZ, normalized diagonal input, acceleration and solid arena boundaries. Camera-relative input follows an orthographic camera at 39 degrees. Character and target graphics are original procedural placeholder sprites, facing the camera with depth-based sorting and ground shadows.

Player rolls spend actual concentration points with an increasing series cost: **50, 75, 100, 125, …**. Each accepted roll adds 25 points to the next roll’s price. After **two seconds from the end of the last accepted roll**, the price resets to 50. Rejected inputs do not spend points, advance the series or extend its timer. Damage and stance changes do not erase the surcharge; revive clears it. Enemy rolls retain their flat 20-point cost. A roll cannot start while attacking, disrupted, or on cooldown. It can start at any concentration load. If the roll leaves the load at 100%, balance breaks for 1.25 seconds as soon as the roll ends. Rolling reduces the damage hitbox rather than granting invulnerability. Rejected attempts do not spend load. The cost restarts the two-second recovery delay. Tune the Player component’s `rollPressure`, `rollChainPressureIncrement` and `rollChainResetDelay` in the scene Inspector.

Melee has distinct anticipation, attack and recovery phases. Normal player attacks use a 0.09-second active strike and 0.12-second recovery; enemy swing animation takes 0.1 seconds with 0.5-second recovery (25% shorter when aggressive). The shared **0.28-second parry window** remains unchanged. The ordinary player windup lasts 0.21 seconds; saved enemy windups are 0.24 seconds (0.285 for the second duelist). The weapon draws back and settles into a short held warning pose without dealing damage, then releases with a fast cubic sweep/thrust and slower follow-through. Recovery briefly holds the finish before easing back to guard. Body recoil and forward lean reinforce these phases. Damage remains restricted to the existing attack phase; lengthening anticipation does not extend the damage window. Attack direction is shown by the fighter’s body pose and sword in hand. The blade follows the projected ground-plane heading in every direction, winds back and sweeps across the locked attack heading while the body leans into the strike. Range indicators are hidden by default; enable Show Attack Range Indicators on ArenaDemo or DuelArena in the Inspector to show them. Movement slows during attacks; facing locks for each swing. Standard swords use a 70-degree sector (35 degrees to either side) for both the player and enemies. Targets within that sector receive one hit per swing, flash, lose health and are knocked back. Solid obstacles block hits. Dummies respawn after 2.5 seconds. A short input buffer accepts the next attack near the end of recovery. Rolls use a locked direction, duration and cooldown and collide with walls.

Every ordinary attack by the player or an enemy costs **15 concentration points**, charged once when its windup starts, including misses and interrupted swings. Each combo follow-up pays separately. An ordinary attack can start at any concentration load and spends up to the remaining reserve. A parried attack at 100% load breaks the attacker's balance for 1.25 seconds. Tune `PlayerMotor.attackPressure` or `EnemyBrain.attackPressure` independently. Powerful lunges retain their separate 390-point cost and exhaustion exception; their cost is not increased by the ordinary swing cost.

When opposing attacks meet from the front, **active versus active produces a mutual parry**, while **active versus windup produces a mutual block**. Recovery and idle poses cannot clash. Both fighters keep their health, gain concentration load (45 each for a parry clash or 28 for a block clash), recoil and are pushed apart; a powerful incoming weapon costs its opponent 80 points and strengthens the push. Both attacks are interrupted immediately, including a prepared lunge. Clash resolution precedes emergency guard and is shared by player/enemy and AI duels. A pair resolves once per frame so the second callback cannot turn the same clash into damage.

When an enemy parries a player strike, the player cannot start another attack for **0.85 seconds from that parry**, including a mutual parry clash. The ordinary 0.35-second recoil still ends on time; guard/parry, movement and rolling become available after recoil. Attack inputs during the extra delay are discarded rather than queued. This gives the enemy’s 0.7-second counterattack delay and 0.24-second windup time to beat a fresh player slash. Tune `PlayerMotor.attackDelayAfterParry` in the Inspector. Revive clears the restriction.

## Relaxation stance

Press **Alt / Option** while idle and unguarded to relax: the sword arm stretches to the side, movement falls to **30%**, and concentration recovery is multiplied by **2.5**, on top of the existing three load zones. The ordinary two-second pause after combat still applies. Pressing Alt again lowers the stretch, but preserves the next-action preparation penalty. A normal attack immediately leaves the stance and uses **twice its usual windup**; selecting lunge preparation uses a 1.8-second windup instead of 0.9. The first guard input leaves relaxation and takes **0.2 seconds to raise the weapon** before blocking or opening the normal parry window (ordinary guard remains immediate). Releasing guard midway does not erase the exit penalty. A connecting hit, stagger, roll or respawn cancels relaxation. Enemies enter the same stance when retreating to recover: they move slowly and lower guard rather than receiving the bonus while defending. Their first attack also doubles its windup, and emergency defense cannot skip the slower guard draw.

## Active enemy

Both the player's `PlayerMotor` and each enemy's `EnemyBrain` have an independent **Endurance** setting, with a default baseline of 100 points. A value of 200 doubles the baseline reserve and 50 halves it. Set these values in the scene Inspector before Play; changing player endurance does not rescale enemy settings. Blocks, incoming parries, hits, emergency defense and rolls consume actual points (28/45/22/45/20); concentration meters, screen effects, recovery zones and AI decisions use the percentage of each fighter's own capacity. Passive recovery restores 18 points/second or 28 under guard before the ×1/×2/×4 current-load zone multiplier, so a larger reserve also takes longer to refill. Player revive and enemy respawn preserve their configured capacities. The HUD displays remaining/max STA plus percentage load. Health remains separate at two HP. Batch check: `Parry.Editor.ArenaSceneBuilder.VerifyEndurance` (without `-quit`) tests player 150 and enemy 200 independently, including player roll cost and revive.

Before Play Mode, use **ArenaDemo → Combat settings → Base Attack Radius** in the scene Inspector to set the shared attack radius (0.5–8 world units, default 2.5). It affects both fighters in directional and circular modes, including their indicators and the enemy's engagement distance (2.1 units at the default radius). Save the scene to preserve the setting.

Before entering Play Mode, select the scene object with the `ArenaDemo` component and enable **Combat settings → Circular Attacks** in the Inspector to replace both fighters' attack sectors with full circles. The option is saved with the scene, is off by default and has no in-game toggle. Indicators switch to rings; range, timing, one hit per swing and obstacle blocking remain unchanged. Blocks, parries and knockback use the direction from the attacker to the victim. Batch regression check: `Parry.Editor.ArenaSceneBuilder.VerifyCircularAttacks` (without `-quit`).

Enemy movement and target knockback sweep the full body capsule against solid objects, stop with a small clearance at contact and resolve existing overlaps. This prevents damage knockback from pushing the enemy through walls or scenery, including on long frames. Trigger hitboxes remain available for melee detection.

Defeated enemies leave a darkened, fallen body and ground shadow at the death position. The remains belong to the arena, have no collider, AI, weapon or health bars, and stay after the enemy dies. Bodies remain until the scene is unloaded. Training dummies keep their existing disappearance/respawn behavior.

The enemy periodically asks whether to retreat and restore concentration, sampling **once per second** by default (`EnemyBrain.retreatDecisionInterval` in the Inspector). Retreat probability uses current concentration load: `0.02 + 0.96 * smoothstep(load / 100)`. Against a fully restored opponent, a full enemy reserve gives a 2% retreat chance; half remaining gives 50%; 10% remaining gives about 95%; none remaining gives 98%. Player concentration multiplies this chance by `1 - 0.75 * smoothstep(playerLoad / 100)`: with both fighters at 10% remaining, the chance is about 26%, and with both empty it is 24.5%. The reduction is smooth and never forces the probability to zero. Each decision is random; declining to rest does not cause another roll until the next interval. Long frames do not replay missed questions. Critical danger makes a chosen retreat urgent, but no longer forces a deterministic retreat every frame.

When the enemy has at least **30 percentage points more concentration remaining** than its visible opponent, it enters **FULL OFFENSE** immediately, cancelling rest and suspending rest decisions. It pursues at 1.5× normal speed (with the default aggressive multiplier), uses three-strike combinations, shortens strike intervals by 30%, recovers in 55% of the normal duration and waits only 0.1 seconds in guard. Actual incoming hits, concentration costs, its own stagger and post-block/parry counterattack delays still apply. The mode lasts until its reserve advantage falls below 20 percentage points, preventing oscillation around the entry threshold; then ordinary random rest decisions resume. Equal low reserves do not trigger this override.

Once retreat is chosen, the enemy faces its opponent, lowers its sword into relaxation and seeks a gap of at least 5.2 units or two units beyond the opponent’s reach. It rests for at least 0.8 seconds and until load drops to 35% or less. Reengagement gives a fresh interval before another rest question. A visible opponent with **30% concentration or less remaining** encourages aggressive attacks even at low enemy reserve if the rest decision was declined. An already chosen retreat still runs its course. An off-balance opponent retains absolute priority: the enemy cancels rest and attacks; normal rest questions resume once that opening ends. Its own balance breaks, hit reactions, attack costs and counterattack cooldowns still apply.

After each strike, the enemy waits at least **0.65 seconds before starting another windup** (0.75 for the second duelist), including combo follow-ups and attacks against a broken guard. Emergency defense cannot bypass this cooldown. For each incoming hit it makes one random choice: **50% ordinary block, 50% parry attempt**. The attempt still requires a valid parry window and respects the shared cooldown; otherwise it becomes a block. The choice is not rerolled every frame, and the player's parry is unchanged. Tune `attackInterval`, `recovery` and `parryChance` in `EnemyBrain.cs`.

The enemy HUD reports PRESSING, CAUTIOUS, RECOVERING or STAGGERED. The player can chase the retreating enemy to prevent recovery or use that time to restore their own concentration.

Batch aggression regression check: `Parry.Editor.ArenaSceneBuilder.VerifyAggression` (without `-quit`) verifies that an enemy at 80 load near an arena corner counterattacks without waiting to recover.

For ordinary slashes, the player’s windup and active strike count as an immediate attack threat. For a powerful lunge, visible key-2 preparation makes a nearby enemy choose a defense: prefer a dodge (80%) or prepare a parry. The enemy picks a collision-free left, right or backward escape relative to the observed heading, holds that plan, and starts moving only at release. Preparation does not open a parry window. The active threat and extended reach begin at release. The enemy can initiate counterattacks during player recovery rather than holding guard for the entire attack animation; this also lets it detect fresh strikes during buffered attack chains. Defense against actual incoming hits remains available through emergency guard.

When the player traps the enemy near two differently oriented walls, it can escape with a roll. It probes nearby walls, checks 16 possible full-body capsule paths and favors a clear destination away from the player and surrounding obstacles. The roll travels at 14 units/second for 0.28 seconds, costs 20 concentration load and has a three-second cooldown. It cannot start during concentration break, recoil, windup or unfinished recovery, or if its cost would break concentration. During the roll, guard and attacks are disabled and melee hits are dodged; the visible sprite tumbles and the HUD shows ESCAPING. Movement still respects solid obstacles. Tune the Enemy component's Corner escape fields. Batch behavior check: `Parry.Editor.ArenaSceneBuilder.VerifyCornerRoll` (without `-quit`).

The enemy also rolls toward the player from 3.5–7 units away when it is not tactically retreating. It checks direct and angled capsule paths and stops about 1.7 units from the player without overshooting or crossing their collider. Shorter approaches cover less ground over the same 0.28-second animation. Approach and escape rolls share the 20-load cost and three-second cooldown; the HUD shows CLOSING during an approach. After closing, the enemy commits to fighting and can attack once the existing attack cooldown allows. Tune `approachRollRange` under the Enemy's Tactical rolls settings. Batch check: `Parry.Editor.ArenaSceneBuilder.VerifyApproachRoll` (without `-quit`) covers approach, follow-up attack, shared cooldown and subsequent corner escape.

During windup, the enemy commits until an incoming strike actually connects; simply starting a player attack no longer cancels its swing. With a 0.24-second enemy windup and a 0.21-second player windup, a reactive counterattack has little time to land before the enemy strikes. A parry raises guard immediately and retains its 0.28-second window, making it the more forgiving response. Emergency defense still costs 45 load when contact interrupts the enemy.

Both fighters have **2 health points** and deal **1 damage** per unguarded strike: two landed hits kill, including during a guard break. Dummies keep 3 health. The enemy has reduced hit knockback of 3 (dummies keep 5) and does not respawn automatically. Player hits stun it for 0.23 seconds and interrupt its attack. Both fighters can be hit during a roll if the attack intersects their reduced hitbox. The player retains 0.8 seconds of protection after taking damage. At zero health, movement and attacks stop and the enemy stops attacking. Press R to revive/heal at the center with one second of protection.

The enemy defends every incoming attack while its concentration permits. It attempts a fresh timed parry, subject to the shared anti-spam cooldown; otherwise it blocks. A nearby player attack can interrupt its windup or attack recovery with an emergency defense costing **45 load**, cancelling the swing and remaining combo. An abrupt defensive turn beyond the guard cone has the same cost. The charge is applied before defense: if it breaks concentration, the incoming hit can land. A successful parry does not refund the emergency defense cost. Ordinary raised-guard defense does not pay this extra charge. The enemy turns toward incoming strikes and prioritizes guard during the player's windup and active strike, but can start a counterattack during player recovery. Only concentration break or parry recoil prevents defense outside a roll. Defense is also refreshed when a hit arrives, avoiding an unguarded frame caused by script update order. Tune `emergencyGuardPressure` in `EnemyBrain.cs`.

## Weapons, blocking and concentration

Both fighters carry a visible 2D sword with idle, guard, windup, swing and stagger poses. Hold RMB / K / LB to block; a fresh press opens a 0.28-second parry window. If guard was raised within **0.2 seconds before an incoming windup starts**, that press can also parry through the expected impact plus an 0.08-second margin. Each press can anticipate only one nearby unobstructed attack; long-held guard remains an ordinary block. The extension does not bypass the shared press cooldown or allow a second parry from the same press. Guard protects a 140-degree frontal cone, so attacks from behind still hurt. Blocking slows the player to 45% movement speed. Release guard to attack; rolling lowers guard. While attacking or staggered, a fighter cannot block.

Orange concentration bars display **load**: 0 is stable, 100 breaks defense. Rules are shared by the player and enemy:

Overhead bar fills render explicitly above their gray tracks and slightly closer to the camera, preventing transparent sorting from hiding the fill. At zero load the fill is disabled rather than drawing a degenerate line; at a break the bar is fully red. The HUD's numeric load remains available.

- Block: no HP loss, defender gains 28 load.
- Parry: no HP loss, defender's load stays unchanged; attacker gains 45 and recoils for 0.35 seconds. A press deflects one strike. Repeated presses within 0.3 seconds cannot reopen the window. Parrying restarts the defender's recovery delay.
- Unguarded hit: HP damage and 22 load.
- At 100 load: guard drops and attacks/movement are interrupted for 1.25 seconds. Unblocked hits still deal 1 damage. Breaking a block protects HP on that particular strike. The enemy follows up a guard break promptly instead of waiting through its guard phase.
- Load recovers at 18/second without guard or 28/second with guard only after **more than two seconds without combat pressure**. Starting an attack, blocking, parrying, taking a hit, being parried or using emergency defense restarts the delay. Releasing/re-raising guard cannot bypass it. After a break, load explicitly resets to 35; this partial reset is separate from passive recovery.

Concentration recovery uses three current-load zones: **0–33%: ×1**, **33–67%: ×2**, **67–100%: ×4**. These multiply the base 18 points/second without guard or 28 with guard, using each fighter’s own endurance capacity. A frame crossing a boundary uses the slower rate for the remaining part of that frame. The two-second combat delay and balance-break rules still apply. There is no accumulated fatigue or lasting recovery penalty from prior combat; AI decisions also no longer depend on fatigue.

The enemy uses the same parry window and direction checks, so its newly raised guard can deflect a player attack. Force expensive emergency defenses during its windup/recovery, parry its attacks and punish concentration breaks or recoil. Combat feedback shows BLOCK, PARRY, DEFLECTED and GUARD BROKEN, with weapon-clash sparks. Death/respawn hide and restore weapons; R resets the player's concentration and protection state.

The player's missing health progressively desaturates the scene toward black and white, without extra darkening or a red filter. Concentration load adds pulsing lens distortion, chromatic channel separation resembling 3D glasses, and camera shake, with the strongest effects at guard break. Both effects ease toward the current combat state; healing/reviving with R clears them. Camera shake is applied separately from the smooth follow position, so it does not accumulate drift. The HUD stays sharp and readable. These effects use a runtime URP volume and preserve the arena's existing bloom.

## Combat audio

Eight original synthesized WAV effects cover committed swings, damaging hits, ordinary blocks, timed parries, rolls, concentration breaks, death and player revive. Both fighters use the same event hooks; blocked/invulnerable hits do not play damage sounds, rejected rolls are silent, and player swing audio plays once per active swing rather than every hit-scan frame. Concentration break audio plays once per transition. A shared arena-level Combat audio object pools 16 partially positional AudioSources, varies pitch slightly, limits same-cue retriggers and exposes a Volume slider during Play. The saved Main Camera already has an AudioListener. Clips are retained in builds through `Assets/Resources/Audio/Combat`; replace those WAV files to customize the sounds. Regenerate with `python3 Tools/generate_combat_audio.py`. Batch integration check: `Parry.Editor.ArenaSceneBuilder.VerifyAudio` (without `-quit`).

## Blood, parry sparks and sprite lighting

Every damaging direct hit against the player emits 64 dark red blood droplets, regardless of concentration. Enemy blood still requires broken concentration. Lethal hits emit blood too; blocks, parries, dodges and invulnerable hits do not. Droplets follow the attack direction, fall under gravity and leave small stains when they hit solid floors or walls. Blood traces ignore the player's controller so it cannot swallow droplets or prevent floor stains. A larger stain appears beneath the impact. Both fighters share an arena-level pool, so death/respawn does not erase blood and pools are not duplicated. The effect is capped at 192 airborne droplets and 384 stains; stains last 25 seconds and fade over the last five. These transient effects are initialized at runtime by `BloodSplatter.cs`.

Successful parries by either fighter emit 42 bright golden metallic streaks, a 1.5-unit soft impact flash and a 0.18-second golden point-light pulse (peak intensity 18). Ordinary blocks emit 14 dim orange-red sparks, a smaller 0.7-unit flash and a 0.1-second orange-red light pulse (peak intensity 4.5). Both illuminate nearby lit level surfaces. Each fighter reuses its emitters; particles travel in world space, fall under gravity and fade. Block audio is a short damped low impact at reduced volume; parry audio has a sharp, bright metallic ring with a longer tail. Regenerate only these cues with `python Tools/generate_combat_audio.py Block Parry`.

Player, enemy, training dummies and swords now use `Parry/Sprite Normal Lit`, a transparent URP shader for 3D directional and additional lights. It handles sprite tint, flip, rotation, depth sorting, directional-light shadows and metallic sword highlights. The arena has warm/cool point lights, and parry flashes illuminate the normal-mapped surfaces.

The maps in `Assets/Resources/Combat/*_normal.png` match the existing code-drawn sprite silhouettes: rounded heads, cloak folds, target relief and sword bevels. These are **linear uncompressed RGB vector textures**, decoded explicitly by the shader; keep their texture type Default rather than switching to Unity's packed Normal Map format. `CombatNormalMapImporter` enforces the required linear, point-filtered, clamp, no-mipmap import settings. Regenerate with `python3 Tools/generate_normal_maps.py`. If replacing the sprite art, supply matching normal maps too. Shader and map resources are retained in builds via Resources.

The player arena starts with four enemies. Enemy automatic respawning is disabled by default (`EnemyBrain.autoRespawn`); defeated enemies stay dead until the scene restarts. Training dummies retain their automatic reset, and R still revives the player manually.

Nearby enemies fighting the player coordinate when at least two living, combat-ready allies can see the player within the coordination radius. They spread around the player and wait up to 0.35 seconds for an encirclement, then launch ordinary slashes with a shared impact time. Recovery, concentration costs, defensive reactions and retreat still apply. An unavailable ally or blocked flank cannot hold up an attack indefinitely. Tune `coordinationRadius` and `surroundWait` on EnemyBrain.

When a nearby visible ally is inside the player’s committed attack arc, other enemies intervene for `allyProtectionDuration` (default 1.2 seconds): they stop resting, close in and counterattack without waiting for a group formation. Personal defense and exhaustion take priority; attack costs, recovery and post-defense delays still apply. Merely preparing a lunge does not trigger intervention.

Enemies are instances of `Assets/Resources/Combat/Enemy.prefab`: behavior, collision, sprite and ground shadow belong to one prefab root. Clicking the sprite or shadow selects the enemy root (`SelectionBase`); move, duplicate or delete the root to act on the whole enemy. Scene-specific positions, colors and combat targets remain instance overrides. `Parry > Unify Enemies Into Prefab` converts existing enemies in all three scenes while retaining their configuration. New arena builds instantiate the same prefab.

Consecutive ordinary slashes alternate the sword windup, sweep direction and body twist when the next attack starts within 0.18 seconds of the previous recovery ending. This applies to player and enemy combos. Pauses and interruptions reset the direction; powerful lunges keep their forward thrust animation.

Powerful attacks require combat warmup for both player and enemies. Each ordinary swing that contacts a living opponent (including its block or parry) earns one unit, at most once per swing; misses earn none. A successful parry, including a parry clash, earns two units. Three units unlock one lunge, with a maximum of six units (two lunges). Releasing a powerful attack spends three units in addition to its 390 concentration cost, even on a miss or interruption; cancelling preparation spends no warmup. Respawn clears warmup. The cyan bar above each fighter shows stored warmup, and the player HUD shows the exact count and available powerful attacks. Pressing 2 without a full charge keeps normal mode and displays a reminder.

Enemies enter the relaxation stance only while retreating beyond `max(safeDistance, opponent reach + 2) + 0.5` units. Closing to `max(safeDistance, opponent reach + 2)` ends relaxation immediately, even without an incoming attack or clear line of sight. They retreat with their weapon ready until the safe gap is restored; the extra 0.5-unit entry margin prevents rapid stance toggling.

If an enemy spends five seconds trying to retreat without safe recovery, it abandons the attempt and fights aggressively for at least three seconds despite low concentration. One continuous second of safe rest resets the pursuit timer; brief gaps do not. Attack costs, guard reactions and exhaustion rules remain in force. Tune `maximumRecoveryAttempt` and `forcedCombatDuration` on EnemyBrain.

Ordinary sword slashes automatically step 0.3 units along the locked attack heading during the final 0.1 seconds of windup. Player CharacterController and enemy capsule movement stop the step at solid obstacles. Each combo swing takes its own step, including reversed slashes. Powerful lunges retain their separate movement. Tune `swordStepDistance` on each fighter (zero disables it).

Powerful lunges advance 1.8 units. Ordinary sword steps and lunges give nearby enemies ahead of the advancing fighter a small backward impulse, once per enemy per attack. A lunge gives a stronger impulse and a 0.2-second pause in pursuit (ordinary step: 0.12 seconds). Reactions require actual movement and a clear path, respect dodges and solid collisions, and do not replace stronger existing knockback. Friendly enemies do not push each other with attack steps.

Zoom also changes camera pitch smoothly: close views use 28 degrees above the ground, the initial zoom keeps the scene’s original angle, and distant views use 58 degrees. Both gameplay and duel cameras orbit their smoothed focus point, keeping the subject centered in perspective and orthographic modes. Tune `closeZoomPitch` and `distantZoomPitch` on ArenaDemo or DuelArena. Camera updates run before fighter presentation so sprites track the changing view.

Group coordination never forces a ready fighter to keep circling in striking range. A lone ready attacker, an exposed target, a player attacking another enemy, or an ally already committed to a strike triggers an independent ordinary slash. Only multiple ready fighters without an immediate opening briefly wait for a shared strike; `surroundWait` defaults to 0.35 seconds. Independent attacks still respect recovery, counterattack delays, concentration costs and recoil.

SampleScene and 0 contain two separate 24 × 20 islands (centers at x=0 and x=32), joined by an 8 × 4 corridor with side railings. Empty space separates the islands outside the corridor. A locked door stands at the second island entrance. The first room has one guard who drops a visible golden key on death. Approach the key to collect it, then approach the door to unlock it automatically. Two guards in the second room wait until the player enters its bounds; walking down the corridor does not activate them. Defeated guards stay dead; R heals/revives at the original starting point without resetting room progress. RoomEncounter holds the scene references and shows the current objective. Run Parry.Editor.RoomEncounterVerification.Run in Unity batch mode to check both scenes and the key progression in Play Mode.

Target lock: Tab or middle mouse toggles the nearest visible enemy within 12 units; Q/E cycle enemies in screen order; Esc releases the lock. On gamepad, click the right stick to toggle and use D-pad left/right to switch. A gold ring marks the target. Facing, guarding and newly committed attacks automatically aim at the target while movement remains free. Committed swings retain their direction. Death transfers the lock to another eligible enemy; walls, smoke, a 16-unit separation, player death or respawn release it. Waiting guards on the second island cannot be targeted before entering. Tune ranges on PlayerTargetLock. Run Parry.Editor.TargetLockVerification.Run for the Play Mode check.

Lunge defenses: powerful parries cost 35% of maximum endurance (at least 80 actual points), compared with 20 for a tactical roll. An enemy caught by the blade during a lunge dodge rolls once for an emergency parry (20%), block (30%) or full damage (50%); geometry must actually intersect the reduced rolling hitbox. Emergency defense still respects concentration and parry cooldown. A blocked powerful attack breaks balance. The player can change lunge aim throughout preparation, including while a lunge is queued, but direction stays fixed after release. Hold Shift during preparation to aim manually while target lock is active. EnemyBrain exposes lungeDodgeChance, caughtDodgeParryChance and caughtDodgeBlockChance. Run Parry.Editor.LungeDefenseVerification.Run for the Play Mode check.
